Direction

A record, not a dashboard

The person reading this app has just been told something unsettling about their retirement. Two failure modes to design against: looking like a government form (which the rules forbid us from implying) and looking like a growth-stage fintech app (which nobody trusts with a pension).

So the direction is a service record that speaks plainly. Its ancestor is a passbook — a ledger of periods that either count or don't — but warmed up, set in a typeface built for Indian languages, and written the way a person would explain it to a friend.

Three decisions and why

Indigo, not government blue or fintech teal

Indigo carries authority and a long Indian association with ink and dye, without borrowing institutional colour language we are explicitly not entitled to. It is unmistakably not a .gov.in palette, and unmistakably not a neobank one either.

Ochre for "not counting", never red

Years that didn't count are missing, not dangerous. Burnt ochre reads as incomplete and warm. A klaxon red on a page about someone's pension makes people close the tab, and closing the tab is the actual failure state we are designing against. True alert red exists in the palette and is used once, for "you will not qualify" — nowhere else.

Anek, a typeface built for this exact problem

Anek was designed for Indian scripts, and Anek Latin and Anek Devanagari are metrically companionable. We ship an EN/HI toggle, so a family where both scripts were drawn together is a functional choice, not a stylistic one. It also has genuine character at display sizes, which the verdict number needs.

The signature: the ten-year ledger

Ten slots, one per year. Solid = counting. Hatched = worked, but not recognised. Empty = still ahead of you. It appears once, on the verdict screen, above everything else.

This is the only place the design is allowed to be bold. Everything else stays quiet so this reads. It is also the thing that will be screenshotted, so it carries the whole product in one image — which is exactly what you want from a two-minute video.

Hard prohibitions

No saffron / white / green together. No Ashoka Chakra, no national emblem, no ministry marks, no .gov.in visual language. The rules forbid presenting this as official or implying endorsement, and a tricolour header does precisely that. Also: never communicate status through colour alone — every state needs an icon and a word beside it.

Rules

  • Measure caps at 60 characters. Long lines are where less-confident readers lose their place mid-sentence.
  • Devanagari needs more leading. Use 1.6 minimum where Latin uses 1.55, and test the 44px verdict figure for clipped matras before shipping DUS-402.
  • Mono is for data only — member IDs, dates, reference numbers. Never for prose, never for buttons.
  • One h1 per screen, headings in order, no skipping levels for visual effect.
Performance — this matters on 3G

Two families plus a mono is heavy. Preload only the weights actually used (Anek Latin 400/600/800, Devanagari 400/700, Plex Mono 400), set font-display:swap, and load Devanagari only when Hindi is selected. Give the fallback stack a matched size-adjust so the swap doesn't reflow the verdict number. Budget: fonts under 90KB total.

S4 · Fix: exit date · DUS-202
Demo · mock data, not connected to EPFO
‹ Backहिं

Fynd hasn't recorded your last working day

You don't need them. You can record it yourself — most people never find out this is possible.

What to do

Sign in to the EPFO member portal and mark your own date of exit. You can do this about two months after your last contribution there. Use your actual last working day — the one on your relieving letter.

How long it takes

The exit usually shows within a few days. Your transfer can go through after that.

If that doesn't work

Email your old employer with your relieving letter attached. If they still don't act, raise a grievance with EPFO at epfigms.gov.in.

EPFO calls this your Date of Exit. Useful to know if you search for it later.

Three blocks, same order, every fix page: what to do, how long, what if it fails. Jargon appears once, at the bottom, as a translation.

  • Test at 360px in a real throttled browser before every merge. Not at 375, not at 390.
  • No horizontal scroll at any width, on any route, ever. This is an acceptance criterion on DUS-501.
  • The sticky action bar must not cover content — pad the scroll container by its height.
  • Touch targets stay 48px at every breakpoint, including desktop.
Accessibility & motion

The floor, not the ceiling

  • Focus: 3px indigo ring at 2px offset. Never outline:none without a replacement — this is a PR-blocking review comment.
  • Status: icon + word + colour, always all three. A colourblind reviewer must reach the same conclusion.
  • The ledger bar needs a text equivalent for screen readers: "4 years 2 months counting, 2 years 2 months not counting, 10 years needed." Put it in an aria-label — the bar is decorative without it.
  • Semantics: <button> for actions, <a> for navigation, labels tied to inputs, one h1 per screen.
  • Contrast: 4.5:1 body, 3:1 for text above 24px. Already satisfied by the palette — don't introduce colours that aren't.
  • Language: set lang on <html> and flip it with the toggle, so screen readers pronounce Hindi correctly.

Motion — one animation in the whole app

The ledger bar fills left to right on mount. 600ms, cubic-bezier(.22,.61,.36,1), 40ms stagger per slot, once per session. That's it.

No page transitions, no skeleton shimmer, no scroll reveals, no hover flourishes. Under prefers-reduced-motion: reduce the bar renders filled instantly. Restraint here is what makes the one animation land — and it keeps the app fast on the phones this is built for.
