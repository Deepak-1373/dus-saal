BUILD PLAN — v1

Dus Saal

Check whether your PF years actually count toward a pension — before it's too late to fix.

The problem

Your money moves. Your years don't.

Since July 2026, EPFO transfers your PF automatically when you change jobs. It works — but only if four separate conditions are all true at once. If any one fails, nothing moves, and nobody tells you.

The four conditions: Aadhaar linked and verified on your UAN; name, date of birth and gender matching across old and new employer records; your previous employer having marked your date of exit; and your new employer having made at least one PF deposit. Miss one and you are back to filing Form 13 by hand — except you don't know you need to, because there is no error, no notification, no failed state shown anywhere.

Why this is serious, not just annoying

Part of every PF contribution funds EPS, the pension scheme. You need ten years of recognised service to qualify for a monthly pension for life. Below ten years, you get a lump sum and no pension — permanently.

Those ten years only count if your service history stitches together across employers. And here is the part almost nobody knows: your balance can be completely correct while your service history is not merged. A transfer marked "settled" does not guarantee the service record updated in the same pass. So the portal shows you a healthy balance, everything looks fine, and your pension eligibility is quietly broken.

You find out when you file for pension. At 58. That is roughly thirty years too late to do anything about it.

What we are building

One screen that shows the number the portal never shows you: your recognised service, against the ten years you need. Then it tells you which of the four conditions broke, and exactly how to fix that one.

The one-line pitch

The portal shows you a balance, which looks fine. We show your service count, which decides your pension.

Why now

  • Auto-transfer went live in July 2026 — most people don't know the conditions attached to it.
  • The EPF Scheme 2026 replaced the 1952 scheme on 29 June 2026; EPS 2026 replaced EPS 1995 the same day, keeping the ten-year rule.
  • The member portal went through a week-long outage and database migration in late June 2026 and came back with a new Member Service History page. Rules and screens changed under people's feet this year.
  • Roughly ₹9,330 crore sits in about 30.9 lakh inoperative EPF accounts as of 31 March 2026. Money does get stranded in this system at crore scale.
Do not overclaim this

The ₹9,330 crore figure is for inoperative accounts, which has a narrow legal definition (broadly, dormancy after retirement, permanent migration or death) — it is not "money forgotten by job switchers." Use it as evidence that stranding happens at scale, never as a headline claim about our specific problem. Honesty is a scored criterion. A judge who knows the definition and catches us overreaching costs us more than the stat gains.

Explicitly out of scope

Say these out loud in the video. Naming what you deliberately didn't build reads as judgement, not as a gap.

  • Duplicate UANs. A separate and messier fix requiring the older UAN to be formally closed first.
  • Exempted establishments (companies running their own private PF trusts) — different process, Annexure K, different timelines.
  • Annexure K reconciliation. We flag that service history can lag a settled transfer; we don't reconcile it.
  • Any live EPFO connection. Zero. By design and by rule.

export type CheckId = 'aadhaar' | 'identity_match' | 'exit_date' | 'first_deposit';
export type CheckStatus = 'pass' | 'fail' | 'not_applicable';

export interface TransferCheck {
  id: CheckId;
  status: CheckStatus;
  label: string;          // plain language, user-facing
  labelHi: string;
  detail: string;         // what it means, one sentence
  detailHi: string;
  blamedEmployerId?: string;   // which job caused this
  jargon?: string;        // e.g. "Date of Exit" — shown in parentheses only
}

export interface Employment {
  id: string;
  employerName: string;
  memberId: string;             // hidden by default in UI
  fromDate: string;             // 'YYYY-MM-DD'
  toDate: string | null;        // null = current job
  balancePaise: number;         // integers only, never floats for money
  balanceTransferred: boolean;  // did the money move?
  serviceRecognised: boolean;   // do these years count? ← the whole point
  exitDateMarked: boolean;
}

export interface Persona {
  id: string;
  displayName: string;
  mockUan: string;              // obviously invalid, e.g. '0000 0000 0001'
  scenarioLabel: string;        // shown in the picker: "Exit date not marked"
  employments: Employment[];
  checks: TransferCheck[];
}

// Everything below is DERIVED. Never stored in the persona fixtures.
export interface ServiceVerdict {
  believedMonths: number;
  recognisedMonths: number;
  gapMonths: number;
  strandedPaise: number;
  totalPaise: number;
  monthsToTen: number;
  eligible: boolean;
  failedChecks: TransferCheck[];
}
Two rules that will save you a bug hunt

Money is integer paise, always. Format at the edge, never store rupees as floats.
Service is integer months, always. Convert to "4 years 2 months" only in the display layer. Every off-by-one in this app will otherwise be a date arithmetic bug at 2am.

Design system

Built for a worried person on a slow phone

The user is reading about their retirement. Calm, not clinical. Serious, not alarming. Nothing on screen should look like a government form, and nothing should look like a fintech growth app either.

Palette

ink#0F2E2B
action#0B6E5F
pass#1F7A5A
attention#C2410C
muted#5B6B68
bg#F7F8F8

Burnt orange, not red, for failures. This is someone's pension — a klaxon-red alert screen makes people close the tab. Attention, not panic.

Rule you cannot break

No saffron/white/green combinations. No Ashoka Chakra. No emblems, no ministry logos, no .gov.in-style visual language. The rules explicitly prohibit presenting this as official or implying government approval, and a tricolour header does exactly that. Our visual identity must be clearly ours.

Type

  • Body 17px minimum. Not 14, not 16. This audience includes people reading in bright sunlight on a cheap screen.
  • The verdict number: 40–48px, weight 700. It is the loudest thing in the app by a wide margin.
  • Nothing below 14px anywhere, including disclaimers. Especially disclaimers.
  • Line length capped at ~60 characters. Long lines are where low-confidence readers lose their place.

Non-negotiable build floor

  • One primary action per screen. If a screen has two equally weighted buttons, one of them is wrong.
  • Touch targets 48×48px minimum.
  • Never colour alone for status — always an icon and a word alongside it.
  • Visible keyboard focus rings. Do not remove the outline.
  • prefers-reduced-motion respected. Honestly, just ship almost no motion — the one place it earns its keep is the ledger bar filling on the verdict screen, once, on mount.
  • Real semantic HTML: <button> for actions, <h1>–<h3> in order, labels tied to inputs.
  • Total JS under ~150KB gzipped. No component library. Tailwind plus hand-rolled components is faster to write and far lighter than pulling in MUI.
  • Works with JS enabled but on a throttled 3G profile — test it in DevTools before you record the video.

Conventions

  • Branches: feat/, fix/, chore/ + short kebab name.
  • main is protected. Everything through a PR. Both of you review — even a two-minute skim. It is a scored signal that this was built by a team, and it catches contract drift.
  • Squash merge. Keeps the history readable when a judge opens the repo.
  • Every PR description gets one line: what this does and which screen it serves.
  • Never edit types/index.ts without messaging the other person first. This is the only real coordination risk in the whole build.
Do this before writing a single component

Deploy the empty scaffold to Vercel on DUS-101 and confirm the public URL loads. The single most common way teams lose these things is discovering a deploy problem at 7pm on submission day. Get the URL working while it costs you nothing, then every subsequent merge to main just updates it.

Summary — 236 words, edit freely

Dus Saal — check whether your PF years actually count.

Since July 2026, EPFO transfers provident fund balances automatically when you change jobs. It works only if four conditions are simultaneously true: Aadhaar linked to your UAN, personal details matching across employers, your previous employer having recorded your date of exit, and your new employer having made a first deposit. If one fails, the transfer silently does not happen. There is no error and no notification.

This matters more than the money. Ten years of recognised service are required for a lifelong monthly EPS pension. Service history can remain unmerged even when the balance is correct — so the portal shows a healthy figure while pension eligibility is quietly broken. Members discover this decades later, when it is too late to fix.

Dus Saal shows the number the portal never shows: recognised service against the ten years you need. It then identifies which of the four conditions failed and gives the specific next step — including that you can mark your own date of exit if a former employer never did, which very few members know.

It is better because it converts a silent failure into a visible one, and checks before a claim is filed rather than rejecting it afterwards.

Fully synthetic data. No connection to any EPFO system. Not affiliated with or endorsed by EPFO or any government body.

Honesty

Disclosure is a scored criterion — make it loud

Judges are explicitly scoring whether limitations, mock data and dependencies are clearly disclosed. Most teams bury this in a footer. Put it where it cannot be missed and you gain points that cost you nothing to earn.

Persistent, every screen

A thin banner at the top: "Demo — mock data. Not connected to any EPFO system." It stays. It does not dismiss.

About page, in full

  • All names, UANs, employers, balances and dates are invented. No real person's data appears anywhere.
  • No live government system is contacted, queried, or scraped. No APIs, documented or otherwise.
  • No Aadhaar, PAN, password, OTP or payment data is collected. The OTP field accepts any six digits and validates nothing.
  • Nothing is stored. No database, no analytics, no cookies. State lives in memory for the length of the session.
  • Not affiliated with, endorsed by, or connected to EPFO, the Ministry of Labour and Employment, or any government body. No government logos or emblems are used.
  • The four-condition logic reflects publicly reported EPFO rules as of August 2026. Rules changed substantially this year and continue to change; verify anything actionable at epfindia.gov.in.
  • Out of scope: duplicate UANs, exempted establishments with private PF trusts, Annexure K reconciliation, and any real claim filing.
